# Ivorian mangoes and the 2026 EU suspension

Research notes gathered on 19 October 2026. Every figure below comes from the sources listed at the end.

## What happened

- Since **1 August 2026**, exports of fresh Ivorian mangoes to the European Union are suspended. Agriculture Minister Bruno Nabagné Koné announced it at a press conference in Abidjan on 15 October 2026. [1][2]
- **Cause:** repeated interceptions of shipments infested with fruit flies. [1][2]
- **Interceptions by EU authorities:** 32 lots in 2024, 12 in 2025, 27 in 2026. [1][2]
- The EU tightened its fruit-fly rules for mangoes in 2019. [4]
- 15% of exporters were responsible for more than two-thirds of the rejections. [5]
- The government does not contest the decision, which rests on international plant-health rules that Côte d'Ivoire itself adopted. [10]

## Impact

- Economic threat estimated at over 7 billion FCFA. [5]
- 15 operators and 9 packing stations are excluded from the 2027 campaign. [1]
- Reopening has no firm date: the decision also belongs to the European Commission. [1]

## Government response

- 3.5 billion FCFA for phytosanitary treatment of the 20,000 ha national orchard.
- Over 3 million traps before the next flowering.
- Stricter controls from orchard to exit point.
- Traceability of each lot to the orchard, the producer and the harvest technician.
- An independent audit before a revised plan goes to the European Commission.
- A national unit created at the end of August 2026; mango integrated into the Conseil Coton-Anacarde-Karité. [1][4]
- Target: resume EU exports in the 2027 campaign. [1]

## The pest

- Fruit flies can cause 50–80% production losses in West Africa. [8]
- In northern Côte d'Ivoire trials (2023–2024), *Bactrocera dorsalis* dominated; untreated plots exceeded 75% infestation, while integrated pest management brought it below 5%. [7]

## History

- Origin: the region between northeast India, Bangladesh and Myanmar; cultivated in India for 4,000–6,000 years. [11][12]
- Arab and Persian traders brought it to East Africa in the 9th–10th centuries. [12]
- The Portuguese spread it from Goa to East and West Africa; it reached Africa broadly from the 16th century. [11][12]
- Côte d'Ivoire made mango an export crop around 1980 to diversify away from coffee and cocoa. [13] Exports rose from 71 t in 1981 to over 40,000 t in 2019. [14]
- Côte d'Ivoire is one of the top senders of Kent mangoes to Europe by sea. [15]

## Nutrition (raw mango, per 100 g)

Energy 60 kcal · sugars 13.7 g · fibre 1.6 g · vitamin C 36.4 mg · vitamin A 54 µg RAE (beta-carotene 640 µg) · folate 43 µg · vitamin E 0.9 mg · vitamin K 4.2 µg · vitamin B6 0.12 mg · potassium 168 mg · magnesium 10 mg · copper 0.11 mg. [9]
